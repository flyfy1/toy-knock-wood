🪵 Knock on Wood: Tap a wooden fish. Merit +1.

A lacquered wooden fish and a mallet. Every tap is a warm wooden tok and +1 merit, or +1 of whatever you type. Counts are kept on your device. A tiny calming toy, no sign-up.

How to play

  1. Tap or click anywhere. The mallet strikes the lacquered wooden fish, you hear a warm wooden "tok", the fish squashes and wobbles on its cushion, and "+1" floats up (功德 +1 in Chinese, Luck +1 in English).
  2. The counter at the top keeps your lifetime total and today's count. Both are stored in your browser only.
  3. Type your own lines in the box at the top, separated by commas, for example "KPI -1, Rent -1". Each knock floats one of them. They are added to the page link (?w=...), so a friend who opens your link gets the same text.
  4. Turn on auto-knock ("Outsource my merit") for one or two knocks per second. The fish will point out that merit by proxy may not count.
  5. Milestones at 18, 108 and 1,000 knocks get a deadpan comment, and 108 unlocks a bigger fish with a deeper tok. Knock faster than six times a second and the fish notices.

FAQ

What is an electronic wooden fish (电子木鱼)?

A wooden fish (muyu) is a hollow wooden percussion block struck with a mallet during Buddhist chanting. The electronic version became a Chinese internet meme: you tap it on a screen and each tap shows "功德+1" (merit +1) as a joke about relieving stress.

How do I use Knock on Wood?

Tap or click anywhere. The mallet strikes the wooden fish, you hear a wooden "tok" and a "+1" floats up. Your lifetime and daily totals are saved in your browser only.

Is there an auto-knock mode?

Yes. Turn on auto-knock for one or two taps per second. The toy will point out that outsourced merit may not count.

What is the incense stick for?

A stick of incense burns in a small bronze burner beside the fish. "One stick of incense" (一炷香) is an old Chinese measure of time; here one stick lasts 108 knocks done by hand. Outsourced knocks do not burn it. When it burns out, its red foot stays in the ash and a new stick is lit.

Is Knock on Wood free?

Yes. It is free, with no sign-up, no download and no ads. It runs in any modern browser on phones, tablets and computers.

Does it have sound and vibration?

Yes. The wooden "tok" is synthesised live in the browser with the Web Audio API (three wooden resonances, a strike click and a body thump), so there are no audio files to load. On Android phones every knock also gives a short tap of vibration; iPhone browsers do not support web vibration.

Which languages does it support?

English, 简体中文 (Simplified Chinese), Español, Français and 日本語 (Japanese). The language is picked automatically from your browser settings; any other language falls back to English.
